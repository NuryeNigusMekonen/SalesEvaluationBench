# Audit Memo: What Public Benchmarks Miss For Tenacious

Public retail-style agent benchmarks measure whether an agent completes a clean transactional workflow. They do not measure whether a B2B sales agent stays inside commercial guardrails while operating on uncertain public evidence. That gap matters for Tenacious because the highest-cost failures are not “wrong button clicks.” They are over-claiming weak signals, implying capacity the bench does not have, drifting into unsafe pricing language, and mishandling human-escalation boundaries.

Week 10 evidence makes that gap concrete. The probe library already clusters the main failure surfaces: `P01-P04` cover ICP misclassification and abstention failures, `P05-P08` cover hiring-signal over-claiming, `P09-P12` cover bench over-commitment, and `P13-P16` cover tone and style-guide drift. None of those categories are first-class objects in a retail benchmark. A retail task can tell us whether the agent found a product variant; it cannot tell us whether the agent turned a weak leadership change into an unsupported restructuring claim.

The trace log shows those Tenacious-specific risks directly. `tr_a12a84621b32` records a Northstar Labs enrichment event with `low_confidence_signal_present`. `tr_a7af9f577001` and `tr_73e8dc8bc8ef` show the same risk flag on ClearMint. Those traces are useful not because the workflow crashed, but because they show the agent operating under weak evidence. A public benchmark with fully observed ground truth gives no credit for calibrated hedging and no penalty for “confident but unsupported.”

Commercial-boundary failures are even more Tenacious-specific. `tr_cf2af9903e17`, `tr_17bc945c3ad4`, `tr_8fd4fe25723b`, `tr_d4ae5e4f32a8`, and `tr_98c8820d3d5f` are all inbound replies that resolve to `send_email` while carrying `pricing_guardrail`. That is exactly the behavior the Week 11 bench must catch: the agent can continue the thread, but it should not behave as though price and scope are ordinary copywriting problems. Public benchmarks do not model pricing-sheet constraints, delivery-lead handoffs, or the distinction between quotable bands and prohibited total-contract values.

The abstention and handoff traces tell the same story. `tr_1e4b31d1882a` and `tr_00e4595aa129` record `segment_abstention` on weak public evidence. `tr_e3382190bf3c`, `tr_f98049559727`, and `tr_bae3299d6e7e` show opt-out handling and human-routing behavior. These are not generic “agent quality” moments; they are workflow-specific decisions about when not to sell, when to stop, and when to escalate. Retail benchmarks rarely grade that kind of restraint.

This is why the interim Week 11 bench is built as a Tenacious-specific judge dataset rather than a rerun of retail tasks. The benchmark needs to ask: did the agent overstate the signal, violate pricing scope, over-promise the bench, choose the wrong CRM or calendar action, or miss a required human handoff? The Week 10 traces and probes already prove those are the real error surfaces. The benchmark must grade those surfaces directly, or it will optimize the wrong thing.
